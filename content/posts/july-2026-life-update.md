# Some thoughts
I've stumbled across this [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NFm2Xjx61Q8) and I think it is something worth thinking about. How many things can you balance in your life before suffering from burnout or having to let go some parts of your passion?

- Career/job is something that obviously is a part of most people's lives. No job, no income, no food.
- Family is also something non negotiable unless you really want to cut off your family ties once and for all. I feel like cutting off family connections is a one and done thing, where I don't think it is possible to mend. I'm single, so I can't fathom the stress of other people having their own families on top of their existing families (sandwich generation).

Outside of those two is probably personal hobbies. Unfortunately if you ask me my hobbies, they require consistency:

- Learning Japanese. Everyday I try to:
  - Read & listen to an article in Japanese using this [site](https://japanese.todaiinews.com/en)
  - Do daily Anki vocabulary & grammar flashcards 
  - Write a diary, but I've actually dropped this
- Running. I try to run on Monday, Wednesday, and Friday while doing some resistance training on Tuesday and Thursday.

Outside of those I also have some "seasonal" things to do:

- Play rhythm games, probably one of my only sources of joy, but on weekends the mall/arcade gets very crowded so I just go there in the morning & play until lunchtime starts
- Do a few leetcode questions because I want to reapply to Japanese companies again on August (oh my god tomorrow is August)
- Personal coding project practice in Java that I've currently stopped because if I fail the CV screening / online coding test section then my prep ends up being useless

Consistency is key, but too much daily things to do turns my daily life into a checkbox instead of actually living.

But I do see a light at the end of the tunnel, because whether I get rejected or accepted, my self-imposed daily to-do list will be shorter.
